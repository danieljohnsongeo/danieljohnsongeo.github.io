Project Overview

This thesis research examines the relationship between landscape change and the spread of Chronic Wasting Disease across Louisiana and Mississippi. The project uses GIS, remote sensing, NDVI-based vegetation analysis, and spatial analysis to explore environmental patterns associated with CWD occurrence.

The workflow integrates CWD case locations, parish and county boundaries, vegetation change products, and multi-year remote sensing data to support environmental and wildlife disease analysis.

Abstract

Chronic Wasting Disease (CWD) is a fatal prion disease affecting cervid populations that continues to expand across North America raising ecological and management concerns. Previous research has examined environmental drivers of CWD while relying on static land-cover data and does not evaluate how landscape changes over time influences disease spread. This study uses a quantitative geospatial approach to assess the relationship between vegetation change and the spatial distribution of CWD across Louisiana and Mississippi from 2020 to 2025. Multi-temporal Landsat imagery was processed in Google Earth Engine to generate NDVI composites and perform change detection. CWD case data was analyzed in ArcGIS Pro using spatial overlay and hotspot analysis. Results indicate that most CWD cases occur in areas of stable vegetation with limited association with large-scale vegetation. These findings suggest that habitat stability and connectivity may play a greater role in disease persistence.
